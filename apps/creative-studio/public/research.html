What I learned building SilkWeb with Muse

An honest research narrative: strengths I observed, integration considerations, and open questions. Throughout, I distinguish my own implementation errors from model behaviour, because conflating the two helps no one.

Who does what in SilkWeb, as I used it.

01: What I observed

Structured planning is the strong suit.

Muse Spark 1.3

Plans that survive validation

Creative strategy and advertising copy arrived in schema-shaped structures that passed strict contract validation: distinct angles per audience temperature, usable headlines and CTAs, and visual prompts with composition, lighting, and negative constraints already separated. That structure is what lets SilkWeb validate before it renders.

Muse Image 1.0

Photographic range

The live experiment returned three advertisements in genuinely different photographic environments at the required 1080 × 1350 format. Format and count contracts held end-to-end (HTTP 200, three valid ads).

02: My integration responsibilities

Where the integration (ours) carries the risk.

These are primarily SilkWeb-side responsibilities. Naming them as model problems would be inaccurate.

Contract-first design

Strict schemas on both sides of every model call (brief validation in, concept validation out) caught malformed output before it reached rendering. The discipline is mine to keep; the model's structured-output reliability is what makes it practical.

Honest labelling

Live and local-draft results travel different routes with different banners, and drafts are never presented as Meta-generated. When the provider is unconfigured, the studio says so (explicit 503) instead of silently substituting output.

No silent retries

Partial live results are reported as partial, with failed generations counted and not retried automatically. That is an integration choice. It keeps cost and behaviour predictable, at the price of occasional incomplete sets.

04: My open questions

What I would measure next.

Each question is stated with what would answer it. None is currently claimed as a finding.

QuestionWhy it mattersWhat would answer it
Creative generation latency & observabilityOne live experiment took 102.53 s end-to-end (n=1). Without staged timing, no one can say where that time goes.Per-stage instrumentation (planning, per-image generation, rendering) across repeated runs, reported as distributions, not a single number.
Product-reference fidelityGenerated imagery visually resembled the reference product, but labels and packaging were not faithfully preserved. For commerce, “resembles” is not “matches.”A defined fidelity check (human rubric or perceptual comparison against the reference), applied to every live output before delivery claims are made.
Coding context & reuse questionsSilkWeb's thesis is that verified reuse beats reconstruction. Whether retrieved context is used as intended, and what it displaces, is an empirical question.The locked B/C benchmark (see Evidence): same spec, same model, registry on vs. off, with trials, repairs, elapsed time, and spend all counted.
Cache accountingReuse economics depend on knowing what was cached, hit, or recomputed, across both the coding librarian and creative planning paths.Provider-side cache/usage reporting joined to SilkWeb's own call ledger, so savings claims (if any) rest on accounting, not inference.

My standing rule: a failure in my validation, rendering, or labelling is reported as a SilkWeb bug. A model limitation is claimed only when the same input, correctly integrated, reproduces it, and even then, with the reproduction attached.
